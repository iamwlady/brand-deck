# Deck structures

Pick the closest one, then cut what has no content. Slide titles are assertions ("Churn dropped 40% in Q3"), not labels ("Churn").

## Investor pitch (10–12)
1. Cover — name, one-line value prop
2. Problem — who hurts, how much (number)
3. Solution — what it does, one visual
4. Why now — market shift / trigger
5. Market — TAM/SAM/SOM, sourced
6. Product — 1–3 screens
7. Traction — the one chart that matters
8. Business model — pricing, unit economics
9. Competition — 2×2 or comparison table
10. Team — why these people
11. Ask — amount, use of funds, milestones
12. Close — contact

## Sales / client pitch (8–10)
1. Cover with client + company logos
2. Their situation (shows you listened)
3. Cost of the problem (number)
4. Proposed solution
5. How it works / timeline
6. Proof — case study, results, logos
7. Why us
8. Commercials / options
9. Next steps — concrete, dated

## Partnership proposal (8–10)
1. Cover — both logos
2. Shared opportunity
3. What each side brings
4. The model (diagram)
5. Pilot scope + success metrics
6. Economics / revenue split
7. Timeline
8. Ask + next steps

## Company / product intro (6–8)
1. Cover
2. What we do (one sentence + visual)
3. Who it's for
4. Key products / services
5. Proof in numbers
6. Clients / testimonials
7. Contact

## Internal update / report (6–10)
1. Title + period
2. TL;DR — 3 headline results
3. KPIs vs target (big numbers)
4. What worked
5. What didn't + why
6. Risks / blockers
7. Plan next period
8. Decisions needed

## Conference / thought-leadership talk (10–15)
1. Hook — a surprising fact or question
2. Context
3–N. 3 key ideas, each: claim → evidence → example
N+1. Takeaways (3)
N+2. Thank you + contact / QR

## Rules
- One idea per slide; ≤ 30 words of body text.
- Big numbers get their own slide.
- Every stat has a source in speaker notes.
- End on an action, never on "Questions?".
